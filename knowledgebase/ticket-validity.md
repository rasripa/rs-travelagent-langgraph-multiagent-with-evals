# Ticket Validity, Peak Times and Routing

All rules described here are synthetic examples for demonstration purposes.

## Peak and off-peak windows

Peak restrictions are assessed on the **departure time of the first train of
your journey**, on Mondays to Fridays only.

| Window | Times | Applies |
|---|---|---|
| Morning peak | **06:30 to 09:29** | Monday to Friday |
| Off-peak (midday) | 09:30 to 15:59 | Monday to Friday |
| Evening peak | **16:00 to 18:59** | Monday to Friday |
| Off-peak (evening) | 19:00 to end of service | Monday to Friday |
| Off-peak (all day) | all services | Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays |

Off-Peak tickets are **not valid** for departures inside either peak window.
Anytime tickets are valid in all windows. Advance tickets are tied to one
specific booked service, so peak windows do not restrict them at all.

## How long a ticket is valid for

| Fare type | Validity |
|---|---|
| Advance | The single booked service only. Not valid before or after it. |
| Off-Peak | Any off-peak service on the printed date of travel. |
| Anytime | Any service on the printed date of travel. |

A ticket remains valid for a journey that **starts** on the printed date, even
if it finishes after midnight. If you are already on board a service that
departed on the correct date, the ticket covers you to your destination.

## Railcard time restrictions

Railcard restrictions and peak restrictions are **separate rules** and both have
to be satisfied.

- Railcard discounts on **Advance** fares are never time-restricted.
- Railcard discounts on **Off-Peak and Anytime** fares may carry a restriction,
  typically before 09:30 Monday to Friday. The exact restriction depends on the
  railcard; see `railcards.md`.

This is why an early-morning Advance ticket can legitimately carry a railcard
discount even though the same railcard would be restricted on an Anytime ticket
at the same time of day.

## Routing

Tickets are issued against a **route**, not a specific set of trains.

- **Route: Any Permitted** allows any reasonable route between the origin and
  destination. Most tickets in this demo are Any Permitted.
- **Operator-specific** tickets are valid only on the named operator's services.
  An Advance fare is always effectively operator-specific, because it names one
  service.
- Breaking your journey (stopping off at an intermediate station and continuing
  later the same day) is permitted on Off-Peak and Anytime tickets, and is not
  permitted on Advance tickets.
- You may not travel beyond your destination, or start short of your origin in
  the direction of travel, unless the route permits it.

## Split ticketing

Buying two or more tickets covering consecutive parts of a journey is permitted,
and is sometimes cheaper than a single through ticket.

- The train **must call at the station where the tickets split**. It does not
  need to be a station where you get off.
- Each ticket must be valid in its own right, including any peak restriction at
  the time that leg departs.
- If a journey is split, Delay Repay is assessed against the delay to your
  **final arrival** across the whole journey, not per ticket. One claim covers
  the journey.
- A split involving an Advance leg means that leg is still tied to its booked
  service, so missing an earlier connection invalidates it.

## Reservations

- Advance tickets always include a seat reservation on the booked service.
- Off-Peak and Anytime tickets may include an optional reservation. The ticket
  stays valid if you travel on a different service; only the reservation is
  lost.
- During disruption, reservations on the affected service are released and seats
  become available to anyone holding a valid ticket.

## Frequently asked

**Is 09:30 peak or off-peak?**
Off-peak. The morning peak ends at 09:29, so a 09:30 departure is outside it.

**I have an Off-Peak ticket and the only train left is at 17:40 on a Tuesday.
Can I travel?**
No. 17:40 falls inside the evening peak, so an Off-Peak ticket is not valid. You
would need to pay the difference up to an Anytime fare, or travel after 19:00.

**Are peak restrictions based on departure or arrival?**
Departure, specifically the departure of the first train of your journey.

**Do peak restrictions apply at weekends?**
No. Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays are off-peak all day.

**Does ticket acceptance during disruption override peak restrictions?**
Yes. When ticket acceptance is in place because the operator cancelled or
severely delayed your service, you may travel on alternative services regardless
of the normal peak restriction on your ticket.
